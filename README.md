<h3>My departure message from 2023</h3>

Since the beginning of 2023, I have quit all computing-related activities, marking the end of a hobby which had been a significant part of my life until then. This includes programming and anything set up here on my GitHub page. I've decided to move on with my life, make new friends, forge stronger bonds with old ones and explore new things (studying finance at university, playing in an orchestra, working at a hospital, trying out kendo and saving up money to travel somewhere). **No new features, updates or maintenance will be performed on any of the projects I (used to) maintain, including security patches.**

I used to be sad that it was over, but now I'm happy it happened. 

— Adrian

<h3>An update from 2027</h3>

I stayed true to leaving behind my childhood obsession of computing. Since then, I made friends and met people from across the world, performed in everything from wind orchestras to J-Pop bands, helped lead music societies at my university, became a staff trainer at my hospital before I was lucky enough to somehow grab two internships, studied kendo for 1.5 years before burning out, lived abroad in London for 6 months, travelled 30+ cities, hit debate competitions across the UK and secured supervisors for various research projects.

Of course, it was a bumpy ride as well. Friendships and family relations crashed, I met people knowing I'd never see them again, I never achieved anything particularly remarkable in any facet of music, kendo or debate, some of my work has made me question what it is I want out of my life, the work I put in for my music societies hasn't necessarily lasted, my summer research project didn't get funded, and I'm still not even sure that my Honours project will be selected after I was sprung with last-minute changes in requirements from my university. 

And sometimes I look back nostalgically on when my life was simpler. When I would go in and out of the same classrooms meeting the same people every day, and spending my free time working on my next computing project. I looked forward to the day I got out of school because it would mean infinite freedom to work on even more computing projects.

I'm writing this update on the day that Claude's generalised Riemann hypothesis proof was confirmed by mathlib and Lean developers. It has made me wonder if I had stuck with computing, whether I would become incredibly more disheartened by the increasing irrelevance of well-crafted handwritten code (not unlike how the mass-manufactured clothes and shoes we all wear has largely displaced artisans and tailors). Looking at my life since I quit, it feels as though I've dipped my toes in many things, yet never really achieved anything - something that my kendo sensei warned me of when I left the kendo club.

But ultimately I'm glad for everything that happened: from my childhood obsession in computing, to my abandonment of it as I left to pursue new areas in my life. I might still be in the process of figuring things out, but I have begun to appreciate this process more, and I wouldn't have it any other way.
